I implemented diagnostic view A (Products & sizes) using the approved synthetic foundation v1.1 and the existing Supabase aggregate API. It inherits the overview’s original sale period, category, product and size filters.

Products are ranked by returned units, with sold-unit denominators, return rates and shares of selected-cohort returns. The product-by-size heatmap displays rates and returned/sold counts in keyboard-operable cells. Small groups remain visible; zero sales displays N/A, while sales with no returns displays 0%. Not reported reasons remain included in return totals.

For Trousers, October 2025–September 2026, product totals and heatmap totals both reconcile to 688 returned / 2,435 sold = 28.254620%, displayed as 28.25%, matching the overview. Cropped Trousers contributes 127 returns (18.46% of Trousers returns), with a rate of 127/385 = 32.99%. Straight Trousers contributes 117 returns but has a lower rate of 117/467 = 25.05%, showing why absolute contribution and propensity should be compared separately.

I verified 22 filter selections against preserved raw sales and return records, independently of the aggregate rows. Product and heatmap sums preserve units, refunds and sales-line counts. The selected Cropped Trousers / XS cell shows 14 returns / 44 sold = 31.82%, with 37 supporting sales lines. Source-to-model count differences are zero; source-to-screen rate differences are zero when rounded to the displayed two decimals.

I tested keyboard selection of the cell and Back to overview, which restores the saved overview filters and comparison mode. Product/size selection updates the visible context and URL and can be downloaded for the later sale_line_id detail layer. Individual record access is not implemented in this exercise. These synthetic patterns support investigation, not causal conclusions.
